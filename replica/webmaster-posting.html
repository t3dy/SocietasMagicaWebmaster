About the Organization

The Societas Magica is an international scholarly society for academics working in the history of magic, witchcraft, and related practices across cultures and time periods. Founded in 1994, the society has approximately 450 members across five continents — historians, anthropologists, literary scholars, classicists, and religious studies scholars who study magic as a serious subject of academic inquiry.

The society publishes a biannual newsletter, sponsors sessions at the International Congress on Medieval Studies at Kalamazoo, maintains a membership directory, and collects annual dues. The website is the primary administrative and communications infrastructure of the organization.

The Role

We are seeking a technically capable webmaster to take ownership of the society's web presence. The incoming webmaster will stabilize the current site, implement a set of quality-of-life improvements for officers and members, and develop new scholarly resources that serve the field more broadly.

This is a part-time contract position, suitable for a freelancer or independent developer. The right candidate is technically solid, self-directed, and comfortable working asynchronously with a volunteer board of academic officers. You do not need a background in medieval studies — but you should be curious about the community you're serving and committed to building something they genuinely use.

Current stack: Custom PHP application on shared hosting. MySQL database. PayPal payment integration. Mailman email list. The incoming webmaster will assess and potentially migrate to a more maintainable platform (WordPress, Outseta, or similar) in the medium term.

What We're Looking For

The Societas Magica is run almost entirely by volunteers who are professional academics — not technologists. The right candidate for this role is someone who can translate between the technical world and the academic one: who understands what a CFP deadline is and why it matters, who can explain a server error to a classics professor without condescension, and who takes satisfaction in building reliable infrastructure for a community that genuinely needs it.

Experience with academic or scholarly organizations is a plus but not required. Curiosity about the subject matter — the history of magic, ritual, and related practices — is welcome. What matters most is technical reliability, clear communication, and the initiative to identify and fix problems before the officers notice them.
