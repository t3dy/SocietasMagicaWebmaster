Societas Magica — Site Work Action Map

How to use this map

This is a breakdown of all the work needed to take over, stabilize, and improve the Societas Magica website. Each card below represents a distinct task. Click any card to expand and see details, technology options, effort estimates, and links to full implementation guides.

Tasks are organized by priority (fixing broken things first) and effort (quick wins before big projects). Cards are color-coded to show whether each task is Essential or Nice to Have.

1. Fixing Existing Functionality

The site has known broken pages and outdated content. These fixes must happen first — they're the foundation for everything else.

Restore Contact Page (404)
Essential Critical
▼

The contact page returns HTTP 404. Visitors and media contacts cannot reach the organization. This is a basic credibility issue.

Why This Matters
A broken contact page signals that the organization is inactive or neglectful. Journalists, prospective members, and institutional partners all expect a working contact form and officer directory.

What to do: Either restore the original URL (if it was just moved) or rebuild the page from the officer data in the database. See the "Officer Directory Self-Management" action (§5 in the proposals doc) for a better long-term solution.

⏱ 1–2 hours (quick fix)

Stabilize PayPal/Payment Processing
Essential Critical
▼

PayPal IPN recently broke, causing memberships to not activate after payment. The webhook endpoint is fragile and depends on PHP knowledge to debug.

The Problem
When PayPal IPN fires, a silent error in the PHP callback script means members pay but don't get activated. Nobody knows until members complain. This is a fundamental revenue and membership management failure.

Immediate fix: Get the PayPal IPN endpoint working again. Test it.

Better fix: Replace PayPal IPN with Stripe Billing, which has better webhook reliability and native renewal management. See §2 in PROPOSALS.md.

⏱ 2–4 hours (diagnosis) + 1 day (replacement)

Officer Directory Self-Management Form
Essential
▼

Currently, updating the officer directory requires editing files or database records. When someone becomes president, the page might not update, or it might break. A form ensures consistency and allows self-service updates.

Why This Prevents Future Breakage
The 404 contact page probably happened because a file was renamed or moved during an officer transition. A database-backed form prevents this.

Implementation: One form per officer role (Name, Institution, Email, Profile URL). The contact page template pulls from the database and renders automatically. Any officer with admin access can update their own entry.

⏱ 2–3 hours

Integrate Member Email List with Membership DB
Essential
▼

The "member email list" (likely a separate Mailman or Listserv) is not synced with the website's member database. When someone joins, they're added manually to the list. When they lapse, they stay on it. Two systems, zero coordination.

The Fix
Member email list = site membership. Active members automatically receive announcements. Lapsed members stop receiving them. A single source of truth.

If using Outseta/Wild Apricot: They include integrated email with automatic member status sync. This becomes automatic.

3. Long-Term Labor-Intensive Improvements (2–6 months)

These transform the site from a maintenance burden into a field resource. They require editorial direction and sustained volunteer effort, but they address the underlying problem: declining attendance and interest.

Create Living Bibliography of Magic Studies
Nice to Have Field Builder
▼

Magic studies is deeply interdisciplinary. Relevant publications appear across history, religious studies, anthropology, classics, literature, and many other fields. There is no curated, searchable bibliography of new publications. The Societas is the natural home for one.

Why This Matters for Attendance
A living bibliography makes the Societas a destination scholars visit regularly. It's searchable by period, region, and subject. Non-members can access it (driving new membership). RSS feed lets scholars subscribe to new additions in their specialty.

Implementation: WordPress custom post type + FacetWP, or Airtable with embedded search. A volunteer editor maintains it, adding ~10 new publications per month.

⏱ 1–2 days build + 5–10 hours/month editorial

Read full guide
Dissertation Registry & Grad Student Hub
Nice to Have Membership Growth
▼

Graduate students are the future of conference attendance. The site currently has no hook into that community. A searchable registry of dissertations in progress and completed creates that connection.

Why This Drives Attendance
A grad student working on Paracelsus can find others working on similar topics. They join the Societas early in their academic trajectory. Those relationships become conference attendance in years to come.

Fields: Author, institution, advisor, title, status (in progress / completed), abstract, tags (period, region, subject), "open to correspondence" checkbox.

⏱ 1–2 days build (shares infrastructure with bibliography) + minimal ongoing

Ars Magica Blog — Editorial Program
Nice to Have Visibility + Community
▼

The blog has one post from 2021. A dormant blog signals a dormant organization. With a committed editorial team, 6–8 posts per year become shareable content and a reason for scholars to follow the Societas.

Content Types That Work
Research previews (800–1,200 words), source spotlights, pedagogical posts ("How I teach X"), conference reports, new book forums, dissertation spotlights. The dissertation spotlight is especially high-leverage: it recruits grad students.

Effort: Two editors, rotating solicitation duties. ~3–4 hours per post (solicitation, editing, publication). Monthly calendar of themes.

Open-Access Working Papers (via Zenodo)
Nice to Have Field Infrastructure
▼

Many scholars have conference papers, grant reports, or draft chapters that are too substantial to be blog posts but not ready for journal submission. These currently sit on hard drives and are never cited. A working paper repository gives them a permanent, citable home.

Why This Matters
Papers hosted with Societas branding get cited. Those citations carry the organization's URL. Over time, this is significant organic visibility and positions the Societas as field infrastructure.

Implementation: Create a Zenodo community (free, via CERN). Members upload directly to Zenodo and select the Societas community. Papers mint free DOIs and are citable.

⏱ 1 hour setup + minimal ongoing (mostly editorial vetting)

Implementation Roadmap

Phase 1 (Weeks 1–4): Restore contact page, stabilize PayPal, update stale content, build member dashboard and officer form, set up role-based access. Goal: Site stability and basic usability.

Phase 2 (Weeks 5–12): Newsletter upload interface, email list integration, blog editorial program. Goal: Reduce webmaster burden, maintain active content flow.

Phase 3 (Months 4–6): Living bibliography, dissertation registry, conference calendar, rich member profiles. Goal: Make the site a field resource, drive attendance and engagement.

Phase 4 (Ongoing): Working papers, expert directory, virtual events. Goal: Long-term visibility and community building.
