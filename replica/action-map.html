1. Foundation Work — Get Things Working Reliably

The starting point: protect the existing site with backups, restore anything that needs attention, and bring content current so the organization makes the impression it deserves. These are the tasks that build the stable platform everything else rests on.

Establish Backup & Recovery Process
Essential Do First

Before any changes are made, the current site should be backed up — the database, all uploaded files, and server configuration. Thirty years of newsletters, member data, and content deserve a safety net.

A daily automated backup to a second location takes 2 hours to set up and costs $3–5/month. It's the single most valuable thing a new webmaster can do on day one.

Verify SSL and Security Settings
Essential

The site needs to run on HTTPS. If it's not already secured, modern browsers display a "Not Secure" warning. Google also deprioritizes non-HTTPS sites in search results — meaning scholars looking for magic studies resources are less likely to find it.

SSL certificates are free through Let's Encrypt. Most hosts offer one-click configuration. Takes 30 minutes.

Restore Contact Page (404)
Essential Urgent

The contact page is currently returning an error. Anyone trying to reach the organization — a journalist, prospective member, or conference organizer — reaches a dead end instead of the officer directory.

Restoring this page also means rebuilding it in a way that doesn't break again during the next leadership transition.

Stabilize PayPal/Payment Processing
Essential Urgent

PayPal's payment notification system recently stopped communicating with the site. Members who paid their dues were not being activated. Diagnosing and restoring this gets the membership pipeline working again.

The good news: this is likely a narrow technical issue. Once diagnosed, fixing it is usually a matter of hours. The longer-term path is migrating to Stripe, which is more reliable.

Bring Content Up to Date
Essential

Multiple pages show "Updated: January 12, 2012." The organization has been active and growing — the website should reflect that. Officer bios, conference listings, membership benefits, and publication info all need a refresh.

This is high-visibility work. A new webmaster who brings everything current in their first month sends a clear signal that the organization has new momentum.

Assess Member Directory State
Essential

The member directory is behind a login, but its current state is unknown. How many members are listed? Does search work? Can members update their own profiles? These answers shape every future improvement.

The directory is the heart of member-to-member connection in the organization. Understanding exactly what it can and can't do right now is the starting point for making it much better.

Verify Newsletter PDF Archive
Nice to Have

The Societas Magica newsletter goes back decades. All 44 issues are hosted on the current server. Before any migration, verify that all PDFs are accessible and make a backup.

This is one of the most valuable historical assets the organization has. A quick audit now prevents any disruption during a future migration.

2. Empower Officers and Editors (Weeks 3–8)

The goal here is to give the people running the organization — the newsletter editor, the treasurer, the officers — the tools to do their jobs without needing to go through the webmaster every time. A well-run site gets better when more people can contribute directly.

Make the Site Mobile-Friendly
Essential

The current site was designed for desktop screens. More than half of all web traffic now comes from phones and tablets. Scholars browsing on mobile get a cramped, hard-to-navigate version of the site — or can't use it at all.

Mobile responsiveness also affects how Google ranks the site. Most platform migrations (WordPress, Outseta) solve this automatically.

Build Member Management Dashboard
Essential

The treasurer and officers need clear visibility into membership health — how many active members, who is coming up for renewal, who has lapsed — without needing the webmaster to run database queries for them.

A simple admin view: active members, recent renewals, expiring soon, lapsed this year, new this month, plus CSV export for board reports. This changes how officers relate to membership data.

Officer Directory Self-Management Form
Essential

Officer transitions are a natural part of any academic society. Right now, updating the directory requires editing files directly — a step that's easy to miss during a busy handoff. A form lets incoming officers update their own entries without involving the webmaster.

This is also how the 404 contact page gets fixed permanently. When officers own their own data, transitions are seamless.

Set Up Role-Based Admin Access
Nice to Have

Right now, publishing anything to the site requires going through the webmaster. Giving the newsletter editor, blog editor, and treasurer access to their own areas — and only their own areas — means content gets published faster and the webmaster's time is freed up for real development.

Roles to define: Newsletter Editor, Blog Editor, Treasurer (read-only financial view), Officer (edit own profile). Each person can do exactly what they need, nothing more.

Newsletter Upload & Publish Interface
Essential

The newsletter editor is a volunteer who shouldn't need FTP credentials and server knowledge to do their job. A simple upload interface — PDF, season, year, issue number, click Publish — puts them fully in control of their own publication.

Publishing can also automatically send an email announcement to the membership list. One action, two results: site updated, members notified.

Sync Member Email List with Membership Database
Essential

The member email list and the membership database are currently two separate systems. When someone joins, they need to be added to both manually. When they lapse, they stay on the email list. Connecting them creates one source of truth and eliminates manual coordination.

Active members get announcements automatically. Lapsed members are removed when their membership expires. New members are added the moment they join.

Automated Membership Renewal Reminders
Nice to Have Community Builder

Most membership lapses happen not because someone decided to leave, but because they got busy and forgot to renew. Automated reminders — 30 days before expiration, 7 days before, and on the expiration date — are the simplest way to keep members connected.

Studies of membership organizations consistently show that renewal reminders alone lift renewal rates by 15–25%. This is community care made automatic.

3. Build a Scholarly Resource (Months 2–6)

These are the projects that turn the site from an organizational brochure into a genuine destination for scholars in the field. Each one gives researchers a reason to visit regularly, to bookmark the site, to recommend it to colleagues and students. The goal is to make the Societas Magica website something the field relies on — the way it already relies on the Kalamazoo sessions.

Create a Living Bibliography of Magic Studies
Nice to Have Community Builder

Magic studies publications are scattered across history, religious studies, classics, anthropology, and literary studies. There's no single curated, searchable source. A living bibliography on this site would become an essential reference for everyone working in the field.

Members contribute entries. Searchable by period, region, and subject. Open to non-members — which brings new scholars into contact with the organization.

Dissertation Registry & Graduate Student Hub
Nice to Have Community Builder

Graduate students working on magic studies often don't know about each other's work. A searchable registry of in-progress and recently completed dissertations connects researchers, surfaces potential collaborators, and helps advisors and mentors find each other.

Graduate students are also the future of conference attendance and membership. Reaching them early builds the organization's next generation of participants.

Field-Wide Conference Calendar & CFP Digest
Nice to Have Community Builder

Magic studies scholars present at 50+ conferences a year across many disciplines. Keeping track of call-for-papers deadlines across all of them is genuinely difficult. A curated calendar — with a monthly email digest of upcoming CFPs — would be something scholars bookmark and return to.

Members submit upcoming CFPs and conference listings. The webmaster spends an hour a month maintaining it. The value to the field is substantial.

Rich Member Profiles with ORCID Integration
Nice to Have Community Builder

The member directory today is a contact list. With richer profiles — research interests, current projects, ORCID publications (pulled automatically), and availability for peer review or media — it becomes a collaboration and discovery tool. A scholar looking for a Solomonic-tradition specialist can find one in minutes.

Richer profiles also give members a reason to log in regularly and keep their information current. The directory becomes more valuable the more it's used.

Ars Magica Blog — Editorial Program
Nice to Have Community Builder

The Ars Magica blog has one post from 2021. With an editorial commitment — 6–8 posts a year — it becomes a place for the field to think out loud between conferences. Research previews, source spotlights, pedagogical pieces, conference reports, book forums, dissertation spotlights: there's no shortage of material from active members.

Each post is searchable, shareable, and brings new visitors to the site. The technical work is minimal; this is an organizational and editorial commitment.

Open-Access Working Papers (via Zenodo)
Nice to Have Community Builder

Many scholars have conference papers and polished draft chapters that are substantial but not yet journal-ready. Hosting them through the Societas gives them a permanent, citable home — a free DOI through CERN's Zenodo platform — and the Societas URL appears in bibliographies as those papers get cited.

Setup takes one hour and costs nothing. Members upload papers directly to Zenodo and select the Societas community. The organization benefits from the citations indefinitely.

Virtual Event Infrastructure
Nice to Have Community Builder

Travel costs, visa difficulties, and accessibility concerns mean that some members and interested scholars simply cannot make it to Kalamazoo. Virtual events — an annual lecture, a pre-Kalamazoo roundtable, quarterly working groups — extend the Societas's community to scholars anywhere in the world.

Sessions recorded and posted to YouTube become a permanent archive of the field's conversations. The technical setup is lightweight; the organizational benefit is significant.

Implementation Roadmap

Phase 1 (Weeks 1–4): Stabilize the Foundation
Back up everything. Verify SSL. Restore the contact page. Fix PayPal. Bring content current. Audit the member directory and newsletter archive. Goal: The organization's digital presence is reliable, current, and working — ready to build on.
Phase 2 (Weeks 3–8): Empower the Team
Mobile responsiveness. Member dashboard for officers. Officer self-management form. Role-based admin access. Newsletter upload interface. Email list sync. Renewal reminders. Goal: Officers, editors, and volunteers can manage their own areas without going through the webmaster for every task.
Phase 3 (Months 2–6): Build for Scholars
Living bibliography. Dissertation registry. Conference calendar with CFP digest. Richer member profiles with ORCID integration. Goal: The site becomes a genuine scholarly resource — something field members visit regularly, recommend to students, and cite in their work.
Phase 4 (Ongoing): Grow the Community
Ars Magica Blog editorial program. Open-access working papers via Zenodo. Virtual event infrastructure. Goal: The Societas Magica website becomes field infrastructure — a place the community of scholars returns to for conversation, bibliography, and connection across the year, not just at Kalamazoo.
